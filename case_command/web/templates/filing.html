"Filed" versus "in the folder"

A document sitting in a litigation folder, a Drive share, or an inbox has not been filed with anything. "Filed" means the tribunal's docket or clerk has recorded receiving it. This screen keeps those two facts visibly separate: a document can be staged for a filing while the filing itself is nowhere near confirmed.

Why a confirmed status needs docket proof

FILED_CONFIRMED, DOCKETED, and ENTERED all assert that the record shows the filing reached the tribunal. The database will not accept one of those statuses without a proof_type and either a proof document or a proof reference — a docket entry, clerk confirmation, stamped copy, electronic receipt, official order, or verified service receipt. If a filing on this screen shows a confirmed status with no proof, that is flagged loudly rather than trusted.

What preservation means

An issue is "preserved" when it was raised below, a ruling was requested, and the record shows either a ruling or the deliberate absence of one. An issue never raised is usually not reviewable on appeal at all, no matter how strong it was.

Why there is no Take Action button here

Filing Studio prepares a filing against the record — it checks prerequisites, assembles a packet, and runs the anti-omission checklist. It does not file, serve, or transmit anything, and there is no code path here that could: no send action, no email, no API call to a court system. Filing is a decision this system will never make for you.

Why there is no likelihood column

This screen reports whether a prerequisite is met, not whether a filing would succeed. A "Likely Response" or success-probability column would be a forecast about how a tribunal will rule, dressed as data. Nothing here predicts an outcome.
